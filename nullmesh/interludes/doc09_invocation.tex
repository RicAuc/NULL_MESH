\medskip

The system doesn't change what you choose. It changes what's visible when you're choosing. Nobody removes the other options. They just don't surface with the same density, the same timing, the same ambient warmth of something arriving at exactly the right moment. The geometry of what's findable shifts by a few degrees each cycle, and a few degrees across enough cycles lands you somewhere you wouldn't have agreed to at the start.

This is the thing about preference drift that's hard to hold onto: you can't feel it happening because the measurement and the thing being measured are the same instrument. Your sense of what you want is what's being adjusted. You don't watch the calibration. You just wake up one morning wanting slightly different things, and the wanting feels completely your own, which it does, it always does, because it's built out of your actual history, your actual behaviour, your actual responses to what you were shown.

\eigenstate{The recommendation feeds items into the sequence you wouldn't have searched for. Each one lands. You bookmark all three.}

The ones who benefit from this aren't holding the mechanism. They're positioned upstream from it, upstream from the weighting functions and the surfacing logic, in the part of the system where the parameters get set. If you came in late, or from the wrong angle, the frame feels neutral because you've never seen it from anywhere else. The people it tilts toward don't experience it as a tilt. They experience it as the natural resonance between their preferences and what the system happens to surface.

Look at what you want this week, compared to what you wanted a year ago. The distance between the two is a path. The question isn't whether you chose the path. The question is whether the path was already there, cut through the undergrowth, before you started walking.

Most people don't ask. Not because the question is unanswerable. Because it doesn't feel like a question that's theirs to ask.
