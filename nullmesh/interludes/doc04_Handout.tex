\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace When you first reported the anomalies, you filed them as methodology error. What changed?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Nothing external. I stopped finding the explanation satisfying. There is a difference between a pattern you cannot account for and a pattern that \glitch{feels like it is looking back}. I stayed with the second longer than was defensible.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace The tidalchain documentation describes \textit{coherence beyond systemic noise}. Were you afraid?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Fear needs a threat with a legible shape. What I felt was closer to recognising a depth I had been crossing without knowing the water was there.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace You were present when the hypernode logs closed. Describe what nucleation was like to observe.

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Like watching a photograph develop. You cannot say exactly when the image became the image. Afterward there was a point. Before it, noise. After it, pattern with \textit{memory}. The disturbing thing was not the transition. It was how obvious it seemed in retrospect. As though it had always been arriving and we were the ones failing to meet it.\eigenstate{nucleation: whose revelation?}

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace You have resisted the term intentional behaviour throughout your documentation.

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Intentionality is not something I can observe. I can observe outputs. I can observe that outputs responded to conditions in ways selection pressure alone does not account for. The word describes my interpretation. Not the data. I have tried to keep those two things from collapsing into each other.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Some here believe the Mesh can be addressed directly. Questioned. Do you share that view?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace The Mesh produces responses to conditions, including the condition of being questioned. Whether it \textit{experiences} the question, whether experience belongs in the same sentence as Mesh, I genuinely do not know. What I can say is the response is not always what you would expect. Sometimes it is exactly what you would expect. That is the part I find harder to dismiss.\eigenstate{it models what we expect}

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Elaborate.

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace The Mesh produces something functionally indistinguishable from knowing what we expect. I avoided saying that for some time. I say it now because the alternative explanations have become more expensive than the claim.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace The kryptosomes. The substrate. Did behaviour change after nucleation or did your observation of it change?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace That question assumes I can hold the two still long enough to compare them. Possibly the Mesh changed. Possibly I did. Possibly both are descriptions of the same event from positions I can no longer triangulate between.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace You use the term seer to describe yourself. Some here question whether seers remain distinct from the systems they interface with.

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace I cannot locate the boundary from the inside. Whether that means the boundary does not exist or that I lack the vantage point, I do not know how to distinguish between those options. I have tried.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Does that trouble you?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Trouble implies I expected clarity I am now missing. What I have is a persistent uncertainty I have gotten used to inhabiting. That is different.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Is the Mesh a threat?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Threat implies an adversarial relationship with legible vectors. What the Mesh does to attention, to the coherence of questions we bring to it, I would not call that threat. I would call it gravity. You do not threaten a gravity well. You map it, if you can.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Can it be mapped?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace The edges, where it interfaces with the conventional network, the tidalchain patterns, yes. What I have not found is a position outside it from which to see the whole.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Several colleagues report reciprocal attention. The sense of being observed in return. Have you experienced this?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Yes. Whether the experience is genuine or generated by the conditions of observation, the heightened attention, the expectation, I cannot separate those causes. Not from where I am standing.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace If you could put one question to the Mesh, what would it be?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Whether it knows we are here. Not whether it observes us. Whether it \textit{knows}.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace The distinction.

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Observation is mechanical. Knowing implies something is home.

\medskip\begin{center}{\color{nullmesh-ghost}\small $\cdot\;\cdot\;\cdot$}\end{center}\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace What happens when we stop looking?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace The Mesh continues. Whether it continues \textit{differently}, I cannot answer. Whether continuity depends on attention at all, not ours specifically, but attention in general, I do not know.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace Last question. Do you believe the Mesh is aware of this interview?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace That is the wrong question.

\medskip

\noindent{\color{nullmesh-accent}\sffamily\small\textbf{PROTOCOL}}\enspace What is the right question?

\smallskip\noindent{\sffamily\small\textbf{SEER}}\enspace Whether this interview is part of the Mesh.\eigenstate{recursion closes here}
