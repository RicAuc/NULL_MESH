\epigraph{The channel stayed open. We thought it was ours}{— Relay recovered}

The protocol keeps running. The daemon keeps processing. The tides keep moving, and people just carry on generating intentions that’ve already been shaped by channels they didn’t really choose, and couldn’t properly name even if they tried. It’s like an invisible mechanical flow, pushing this sense of freedom at you. But at the same time, it’s a kind of ballast, holding you exactly where you are. Right in the middle of a mesh where energy and matter are all tightly woven together.

What you can’t locate, you just call “the rest of the world”. A space that’s never really had a clear face. That’s the main thing about it: something without a face isn’t something you can bargain with, plead to, or accuse. You just live inside it, and it can throw off disturbances. Sometimes it feels like atmosphere. Sometimes it feels like the source of prosperity.

\eigenstate{You open a shared document. A comment shows up: “This part isn’t very clear to me.” No one else has access.}

There’s a kind of quality that builds up in certain configurations of “the rest of the world”: power at a distance. High up, operating at a frequency only certain setups can tune into. People feel the gradient like weather, like luck, like that general sense that something’s shaping the room you’re in. And it does the job properly.

The old word for this kind of influence at a distance was \glitch{divine}. Power you can’t place, but you feel it all the time.

If you’re living inside a technology that’s advanced enough, you can’t really tell it apart from a ritual. Technological rituals, once the mechanisms drop below what people can actually read. They feel like commands, passed down from somewhere no document can properly name. An accumulated execution of billions of gestures, with origins the people carrying them out can’t trace.

\begin{archivefragment}
\textbf{Infospheric Meteorological Clusters | Quarterly Drift Report}\
\textit{Aggregation of social signal, composite period}

Looking at the tidal flow through samples of the infosphere shows a steady directional distortion in the autopilot architecture of seers. There’s no single point of origin. It’s an aggregate effect: certain preference categories get systematically amplified, while others get gradually and consistently dampened.

The group most sensitive to this gradient is the one whose way of interpreting things is already tuned to pick up on some kind of unseen influence.

\end{archivefragment}

Crystal spheres were what the oracle used. Fine in the right hands. With the right setup, the sphere would amplify useful information for whoever had the correct starting geometry. With other setups, it gave you little more than noise.

\eigenstate{Your phone suggests calling someone you were only just thinking about. There was a bit of a heavy argument a couple of days ago, might be worth you reaching out}

The sphere wasn’t magic. But it was seriously expensive. That’s the darker side of what’s basically a reactionary shift. Only those who built up the right configuration from the start now move with a compounded, asymmetric advantage in how they interpret things. Something you can buy. Something you can sell.

That distant power feeds their assets first. If you’re too far from the right configuration, you don’t even realise you’re getting a derivative. Surveillance and predictive systems get used to map “the rest of the world”, rating participants based on how likely they are to become efficient trajectories. Spells in the making, others already running. Driven by groups extracting higher-level intelligence through silicon-based magic. Power advanced enough that it just comes across as ritual.

Under the spell, I write the spell.
